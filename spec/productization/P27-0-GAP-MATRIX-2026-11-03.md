## 0. Executive objective

PaySwap must plan against the **economic intent**, not merely the operation vocabulary of one PSP. It must discover and compose current, permitted capabilities across:
- the sender's connected provider accounts and payment methods;
- the recipient's independently verified provider accounts and payout methods;
- eligible marketplace/platform accounts (including Stripe Connect);
- eligible third-party payout products (including Stripe Global Payouts);
- local African payment providers (Paystack first; Flutterwave and MTN MoMo only to the extent their actual connected capabilities prove eligible);
- independently operated PaySwap nodes that have enrolled, attested, authorized provider capabilities and can participate in a permitted route.

The result is a bounded, evidence-backed graph of eligible paths. A path may contain several provider operations and several PaySwap nodes. Each consequential financial leg must still have its own accountable principal, provider connection, legal/product eligibility, authorization, idempotency, execution evidence, and reconciliation.

**Core distinction:** PaySwap may combine valid operations to create a new *execution path*. It may not use extra accounts, merchant payment links, node hops, nominee merchants, or transaction splitting to evade a provider prohibition, licensing requirement, geographical restriction, KYC/AML obligation, sanctions control, or actual transaction-purpose classification.

The product should support:
1. genuine commerce and marketplace payments;
2. approved seller / contractor / supplier disbursements;
3. refunds, reimbursements, and other provider-supported payouts;
4. P2P/remittance only where the actual PaySwap operator and each relevant payment participant have the necessary legal permissions and the selected product/provider expressly supports that use case;
5. transparent no-route and conditional-opportunity outcomes when no lawful, funded, provider-evidenced path exists.

## 2. Transaction semantics and provider policy

### 2.1 A PaymentIntent can fulfil an economic transfer

A sender paying a recipient's genuine invoice, order, fee, or marketplace purchase can economically move value from sender to recipient even though Stripe records a card/customer payment and credits the merchant balance. The PaySwap intent must retain the real transaction purpose and underlying commercial evidence.

A PaymentIntent or Payment Link is **not** a general remittance primitive. Creating a payment link for a friend-to-friend transfer does not make a personal transfer a sale. A route that labels an unsupported remittance as a product/service purchase, or routes unrelated senders' money through an unrelated merchant, is ineligible and must be refused.

Stripe's ordinary Payments product restriction and Stripe Global Payouts are different product/legal contexts. Stripe's Global Payouts material includes fintech disbursements and P2P transfers as a use case for properly licensed fintech businesses. That is not a blanket P2P approval for an ordinary connected merchant account. P27 must obtain Stripe's explicit written/product approval for the actual PaySwap model and must capture the applicable licences/regulated roles before enabling such a corridor.

### 2.2 Required typed intent classification

Before candidate discovery, the accepted typed intent must preserve at least:
- actual purpose and transaction class (commerce / marketplace seller settlement / contractor or supplier payment / reimbursement / refund / regulated remittance or P2P / other);
- sender principal and funding instrument/provider references;
- beneficiary principal, beneficiary type, destination country and payout method;
- amount, source currency, destination currency, exact fee/FX constraints and requested arrival deadline;
- goods/services, invoice/order/contract or other purpose evidence when relevant;
- payer and recipient consent, source-of-funds / KYC requirements, and allowed data disclosures;
- whether partial payment, netting, deferred settlement, split settlement, or refunds are allowed;
- hard constraints versus preferences.

Transaction class must be derived from the real facts and be reviewable. The model may ask for missing facts and explain the classification; it cannot turn a P2P intent into commerce by choosing a convenient Payment Link or metadata value.

## 3. Stripe U2 and U3 — configure the actual platform and account lifecycles

U2 and U3 are distinct; do not collapse them into a single "Connect" toggle.

### 3.1 U2 — existing Stripe account delegated OAuth

Deliver a real, end-to-end U2 Sandbox flow:
1. operator-owned Stripe platform registration and a verified OAuth `client_id`;
2. client secret delivered only through the operator's secret channel into the host-side credential broker;
3. exact registered redirect URI / deep-link return contract, single-use cryptographic state, expiration, denial handling and replay protection;
4. system-browser authorization; exchange the returned code on the host side; never let the model or renderer see the client secret or OAuth token;
5. store the account identity, granted scope, API version, environment and evidence; probe the connected account before marking ACTIVE;
6. support refresh/revocation/expiry and permission-loss revalidation; keep read-only and write grants distinct.

### 3.2 U3 — PaySwap Stripe Connect platform and connected sellers

Configure U3 as a platform integration, not merely another user's API key:
1. Establish that the operator's Stripe account is approved and configured as a Connect platform for the actual PaySwap business model.
2. Decide, document and get operator approval for connected-account types and controller/liability model. Prefer Stripe-hosted onboarding initially where it meets the use case; do not choose Express/Custom/Standard or Accounts v1/v2 from habit. Verify the currently recommended public API and the platform's eligible countries/capabilities at the registration date.
3. Register exact production-like Sandbox callback URLs and a publicly reachable backend webhook endpoint; configure signing secrets through the host secret broker. Use separate event destinations/secrets for distinct environments.
4. Implement account creation / retrieval / update, hosted account-link refresh and return, account-session flows where eligible, connected-account context selection, capability/requirements read-back, disconnection and remediation.
5. Bind account identity to provider-return and webhook/read-back evidence. A seller-supplied `acct_…` string or model-provided account ID never proves access or eligibility.
6. Add per-connected-account capability snapshots: charges, transfers, payouts, card/payment methods, settlement currency, country, business type, verification requirements, restrictions, limits and current observation freshness. Re-evaluate before every consequential step.
7. Support the appropriate commercial Connect charge/settlement patterns only after a source-backed decision: direct charges, destination charges, or separate charges and transfers. Pin who is merchant of record, who bears negative-balance/dispute/refund liability, application/platform fees, transfer group/charge lineage, funds availability, payout timing and refund/reversal behavior.
8. Complete Connect webhooks and authoritative retrieval reconciliation for account/capability updates, charges, transfers, payouts, refunds and disputes relevant to the selected pattern.
9. Provide separate U3 test/live status. Sandbox onboarding never certifies LIVE. Keep all Connect actions behind the existing prepared-action digest, principal/account binding, approval tier, reauthentication, idempotency and reconciliation gates.

**Connect must not be represented as unrestricted P2P.** Its default PaySwap application is a marketplace/software platform for genuine sellers and service providers. Any personal-remittance use requires a separately approved legal/product path.

### 3.3 Stripe Connect is not Global Payouts

Connect transfers and payouts operate in the Connect platform/connected-account relationship and are country/capability gated. They are appropriate for platform sellers and marketplace payments.

Global Payouts is a separate Stripe product for sending funds to third parties. It supports recipients who do not have Stripe accounts, but it has its own origin-country eligibility, Treasury/product access, funding, licensing, recipient verification, country/method availability and API lifecycle. Do not implement Global Payouts by renaming a normal `POST /v1/payouts`, an ordinary Connect transfer, or a PaymentIntent.

## 6. Multi-node PaySwap network and multi-hop route discovery

This is a new network capability built on the existing route planner/compiler, not a prompt-only agent and not an unrestricted relay mesh.

### 6.1 Node model and enrollment

Define a versioned node identity and enrollment/attestation contract. A PaySwap node publishes only provider-evidenced capability offers that are:
- bound to an identifiable legal/operator principal and a declared jurisdiction/role;
- linked to actual ACTIVE provider connected instances, capabilities, balances/liquidity observations and account-level product entitlements;
- constrained by current source/destination countries, currencies, methods, amount limits, funding/payout rules, permitted purposes, required KYC/AML steps and availability window;
- freshness-stamped, signed, expiring, revocable and independently verifiable;
- explicit about whether the node is a mere technical router, payment facilitator, merchant of record, custodian, agent, payout originator or regulated payment institution.

Do not treat a node's self-asserted capability list as provider evidence. A peer's offer is a discovery input; the accepting route authority revalidates it.

### 6.2 Route graph

Represent each operation as a typed edge and each provider account / verified node / permitted settlement venue as a typed vertex. Each leg must carry:
- node ID and accountable legal principal;
- provider ID, connected instance, exact operation ID and product;
- source and destination location, currency, payment method and account references;
- the amount before/after fees and FX, fees, rate/source/timestamp, quote expiry and expected delivery;
- custody/funds-control transition, funding availability and liquidity proof;
- purpose eligibility, jurisdiction/policy decision, KYC/AML/sanctions requirements and evidence references;
- per-leg idempotency key, correlation ID, effect class, approval tier, authorization lineage, webhook/read-back route and finality evidence.

Search bounded paths across multiple operators and providers. Compare provider-native, one-node composed and multi-node routes under the same comparability rules. Hard constraints and legal/product eligibility run before optimization. Ranking should disclose at least total delivered amount, sender cost, FX, number of hops, expected delivery time and confidence, provider/node reliability, reversibility/finality, liquidity risk, required data disclosures and evidence freshness. Disclose search budgets and truncation; never claim all nodes/routes were searched when the search was capped.

### 6.3 Execution is a multi-leg saga, not an atomic transfer

No route is executable merely because the graph compiler found it. Before the first effect:
1. revalidate every node, provider instance, account entitlement, quote, recipient and destination;
2. establish that every leg's funds source and next-leg funding/settlement are available;
3. prepare the exact immutable route and leg sequence;
4. apply Trust/Policy/Governance and any per-principal / per-leg approvals;
5. reserve or otherwise control the relevant funds only where the responsible regulated party/provider permits;
6. execute each leg through its own sealed credential and existing financial authority;
7. reconcile each leg from authoritative provider reads/webhooks before proceeding to any dependent leg;
8. if a result is UNKNOWN, stop dependent dispatch and reconcile first (INV-04); never create a second charge/payout because an earlier response timed out;
9. use only documented compensation (refund, transfer reversal, payout reversal, return, or operator recovery) where legally and technically available. Do not promise atomicity or automatic rollback across external PSPs.

The TL must decide whether the existing P22 composite execution authority can safely express this or whether a new per-leg authorization envelope is required. A multi-hop mission must preserve one immutable route digest but independently scope, approve, consume, and reconcile each consequential leg. Changing an amount, beneficiary, provider, node, fee, FX quote, or settlement path invalidates affected approvals and requires a new preview.

### 6.4 Privacy, security, speed and reliability

Design for least disclosure: pass opaque beneficiary/route references where possible, keep bank/mobile numbers and KYC documents within the provider/authorized node that needs them, encrypt in transit and at rest, and record which principal saw which data. This is data minimization, not anonymity. Each licensed provider and obligated operator still sees and retains the identity and transaction information required by law, contract and AML controls.

Use mutual node authentication, signed/encrypted route offers, replay/expiry protection, key rotation and revocation, scoped credentials, provider webhook signature validation, idempotency, tamper-evident audit, risk controls and dual approval for high-risk/high-value routes. A larger number of hops increases attack surface; do not claim that multi-hop is inherently safer or more private.

Optimize time/cost only after hard eligibility. A local payout rail may be faster than an international wire, but extra node hops can make the path slower. Always compare it with the direct baseline and show the user the trade-off.

### 6.5 Explicit no-bypass law

A PaySwap node must not:
- accept funds into an unrelated merchant account as a pass-through for personal remittance;
- split, relabel, net, route or chain transactions to evade provider, country, sanctions, AML, KYC, licensing or purpose restrictions;
- hide the true originator, beneficiary, beneficial owner, source of funds or transaction purpose from an obligated participant;
- use another operator's API credentials outside the approved principal, account scope, beneficiary and purpose;
- represent a test balance, quote, liquidity estimate, pending status or simulated leg as settled funds;
- use user-owned merchant balances as a general PaySwap pooled wallet without an approved custody/safeguarding and licensing model.

Where the actual purpose or required legal authority is missing, return a typed exclusion or conditional opportunity, not a disguised route.

## 8. Required end-to-end acceptance journeys

All journeys must have contract tests, realistic negative tests, a secret-free machine-verifiable evidence receipt and a clearly stated evidence tier.

1. **U2 OAuth:** real Stripe Sandbox platform client; system-browser consent; code exchange; account read probe; scope binding; token revocation/expiry; denied return; invalid/replayed/expired state; no secret/token in renderer, model context or logs.

3. **U3 hard negatives:** unverified account, unsupported country/capability, disabled payouts/charges, mismatched connected account, stale requirements, revoked platform, missing webhook, account-link replay/expiry and unsupported personal P2P must refuse.
4. **Global Payouts:** prove product entitlement and origin-country eligibility before dispatch; create recipient or hosted recipient-information link; confirm verified recipient; initiate a permitted payout from a funded eligible financial account; reconcile posted/failed/returned/canceled status and trace evidence. Country/method not currently enabled must produce an honest exclusion. If only preview access exists, keep it labelled preview and do not promote to production.
5. **Paystack Ghana collection:** actual test-mode local payment link/checkout; card and each supported MoMo method where entitlement permits; verify the transaction through webhooks and read-back.
6. **Paystack Ghana payout:** bank account resolution; GHIPSS recipient; mobile-money recipient (telco-specific); amount/currency validation; transfer reference; initiate; OTP/finalize when required; webhook/read-back; failed/unknown/reconciled; Bank of Ghana recipient excluded.
7. **Other African Paystack corridors:** country-specific positive and negative tests for Kenya, Nigeria and South Africa; no assumption that Ghana MoMo behavior or currency rules generalize.
8. **Multi-node interoperability:** at least two independently enrolled nodes with distinct provider credentials; a one-hop baseline and a multi-hop candidate; signed/expiring quotes; route revalidation; per-leg approvals and idempotency; interruption at every boundary; timeout/UNKNOWN; node revocation; quote expiry; duplicate/out-of-order webhooks; partial settlement and compensation.
9. **Sender-only / recipient-only provider scenarios:** sender has only a valid Stripe/eligible card method and recipient has only Paystack local bank/MoMo (and vice versa where the route is actually supported). The result must be a provider-evidenced, compliant executable route or a precise no-route explanation with exclusions/opportunities—not a synthetic "transfer" from a PaymentIntent.
10. **Live evidence separation:** no Sandbox receipt can satisfy a LIVE acceptance row. LIVE remains refused unless a separate operator order completes ADR-0019's activation bundle and a live certification suite.
