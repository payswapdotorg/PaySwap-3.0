# ADR-0020 — P27: the multi-provider capability network — transaction semantics, provider products, and multi-node orchestration

- **Status:** PROPOSED → ACCEPTED (P27-0, the architecture gate — the P27-A/B/C/D lanes implement against this ADR)
- **Date:** 2026-11-03
- **Deciders:** resident TL (the P27-0 station)
- **Supersedes:** none. Extends ADR-0007 (the U-form connection architecture), ADR-0008 (the execution kernel and the provider grant split), ADR-0009 (the route-planning routine and its bounded-search law), ADR-0014 (the P21 no-route truth), ADR-0016 (the corridor shape, the composite act and the D13 extend-per-leg discipline), ADR-0017 (the Stripe connector tiers), ADR-0018 (the REAUTH proof). No frozen decision is amended. **INV-30 is added; no invariant is removed or weakened.**
- **Resolves:** the P27-0 gap matrix (`spec/productization/P27-0-GAP-MATRIX-2026-11-03.md`, registration base `5ca077e1b3e3c25897b605e683a4e7da27277cb1`): the economic-transfer semantics question, Stripe U2/U3 configuration, Stripe Global Payouts as a separate product, Paystack/African local rails, and the multi-node PaySwap network.

## Context

P26 closed with the single connected Stripe account (U1) at **634/644 dispatchable** (the held ten are the secrets law), **U2 (delegated OAuth) and U3 (Connect platform) declared UNCONFIGURED with their exact missing operator configuration** (`declaration.ts` L114/L162), Global Payouts absent as a product, Paystack connected read-only with a KES transfer lane that has **no recipient plane** (the recipient is an opaque external `RCP_…`; no `ghipss`/`mobile_money`/`nuban`/`basa`/`kepss` anywhere in `packages/`), and multi-provider routing proven as **discovery** (ADR-0009/0016: `discoveryPermitsExecution: false`, `routePlanningPermitsExecution(): false` — both literals) with the P22 composite act executing exactly one pinned corridor (GHS → USDC(Stellar) → KES) through per-leg dispatch, per-leg idempotency and a custody-continuity gate.

The operator's P27 order: enable PaySwap to discover and execute more legitimate sender-to-recipient journeys by configuring Stripe U2/U3, treating Global Payouts as its own product, making Paystack/African-provider paths real, and orchestrating across independently operated PaySwap nodes — **without weakening the financial authority, provider-eligibility, secret, evidence or live-mode boundaries**. The registration's §1 audit table was re-verified at this station (the acceptance appendix records the row-by-row result, three registration-time drifts caught and fixed, and two external-research discrepancies).

The architectural heart of the order is a distinction the operator stated precisely: **PaySwap may create new execution paths by combining existing, authorized capabilities. It may not make an otherwise prohibited transaction permissible by distributing it across several accounts, providers, payment links or nodes.**

## The decisions

### D1 — The three-plane transaction semantics law

Every money-movement intent in PaySwap is modelled on three planes that are **typed, recorded and never conflated**:

1. **The economic intent** — what the user wants to happen (Alice sends value to Bob), validated by the existing typed intent pins (`@payswap/economic`).
2. **The provider operations** — the concrete, separately-entitled provider products whose composition executes the intent (a PaymentIntent on a merchant account; a Connect destination charge; a Global Payouts outbound payment; a Paystack transfer). Provider operations are facts about *mechanism*, never facts about *permission*.
3. **The regulatory classification** — the transaction class the *actual facts* determine (commerce, marketplace seller settlement, contractor/supplier payment, reimbursement, refund, regulated remittance/P2P, other), together with the jurisdiction, licensing and provider-product permission status that class requires.

A PaymentIntent or Payment Link **can** fulfil an economic transfer (a sender paying a recipient's genuine invoice moves value from sender to recipient even though Stripe records a card payment to a merchant balance). It is **never** proof that the transaction is an eligible commercial payment. A route that labels an unsupported personal remittance as a product/service purchase, or routes an unrelated sender's money through an unrelated merchant account, is ineligible and must be refused regardless of how many provider operations could technically execute it.

### D2 — INV-30: the non-laundering invariant

**Route decomposition, distribution, relabeling or hop-chaining never changes a transaction's purpose classification, regulatory eligibility or provider-permission status.** The classification is computed from the intent's real facts (D3) and is carried by the route as a whole; the **most restrictive applicable classification among the intent and any leg governs the entire route**. Splitting one transfer into many, passing funds through intermediary merchants, distributing across nodes or accounts, or choosing a convenient Payment Link or metadata value changes nothing about eligibility. This is registered as **INV-30** in `spec/architecture/invariants.json` and `spec/architecture/INVARIANTS.md` (status documented-not-expressible; the route compiler's eligibility gates and the P27-D no-bypass battery are the enforcement path, and the census/no-bypass tests cite it).

### D3 — Typed intent classification runs before discovery

Candidate route discovery accepts only an intent that already carries the classification fields (the gap matrix §2.2 list): actual purpose/transaction class, sender principal and funding references, beneficiary principal/type/destination country/payout method, amount and currency constraints with fee/FX/deadline constraints, purpose evidence where relevant, consent/KYC/source-of-funds requirements and allowed disclosures, settlement options allowed, and hard constraints vs preferences. **The class is derived from the real facts and is reviewable.** The model may ask for missing facts and explain the classification; it cannot choose a class. A class change is a new intent (new digest), never a mutation.

### D4 — Provider products are distinct, separately-entitled capability slices

- **U1 (own-account restricted key), U2 (delegated OAuth for an existing Stripe account) and U3 (Connect platform with connected accounts) remain three distinct connection forms** with distinct auth mechanisms, probes and grants; there is no single "Connect" toggle. U2/U3 stay UNCONFIGURED with their exact missing operator configuration until the operator supplies platform credentials through the secret channel — never a dead button, never a simulated OAuth (ADR-0007 D1 law).
- **Stripe Global Payouts is a separate product slice**, not an alias: it requires Treasury (per current Stripe docs), an entitled origin business (current GA: **United Kingdom, United States**; other origins by private-preview request), a funded financial account, recipient creation or the hosted recipient-information form, and its own country/method/currency/limit gates. **`POST /v1/payouts`, Connect transfers and PaymentIntents are never re-labeled as Global Payouts.**
- **The Paystack read-only connection form stays separate from transfer-write authorization** (the audited existing law: `refuseConsequentialPaystackGrant` on the connection scope; `derivePaystackSandboxPayoutGrant` re-derived at dispatch inside the seal, SANDBOX-only, never cached). The recipient plane, collection surfaces and webhook verification are new operations under the same fail-closed endpoint-map pattern — never a widening of the read-only scope.
- **Flutterwave stays read-only and MTN MoMo stays declaration-only** until each has valid credentials, a real probe, a write-grant ADR amendment (the ADR-0008 D2 pattern) and provider evidence. A declared integration or provider card is not a dispatchable capability (INV-13 law).

### D5 — Node enrollment and capability offers

A PaySwap network node is a **versioned identity bound to an accountable legal principal** with a declared jurisdiction and role (technical router, payment facilitator, merchant of record, custodian, agent, payout originator, regulated payment institution — the role is explicit, never inferred). A node publishes **capability offers** that are: bound to ACTIVE provider connected instances (probe-verified, INV-13 — a self-asserted capability list is a discovery input, not provider evidence); constrained by current countries/currencies/methods/amount limits/purposes/KYC requirements/availability windows; freshness-stamped, **signed, expiring and revocable**; and explicit about custody. **The accepting route authority revalidates every offer against its own provider evidence before admission** — a peer's signature proves the offer's integrity and freshness, not the underlying account's entitlement. Node enrollment/attestation and offer verification land in the capability layer (the `ConnectedCapabilityInstance`/observation chain-completeness law is the seam), with structural pins in `route-planning/src/pins/` per the ADR-0009 D2 zero-sibling-import law.

### D6 — The route graph and bounded search

Each provider operation is a **typed edge**; each provider account, verified node and permitted settlement venue is a **typed vertex**; every leg carries the gap-matrix §6.2 manifest (principal, provider instance, exact operation, amounts/fees/FX with quote expiry, custody transition, purpose eligibility, per-leg idempotency, effect class, approval tier, authorization lineage, read-back route). Search is **bounded and count-based** with one truncation receipt per truncating dimension (ADR-0009 D6 law — never "all routes searched" when the search was capped). **Hard constraints run before optimization**: intent window, hop budget, settlement deadline, and the D1/D2 eligibility planes gate admission; only then does ranking compare **provider-native, one-node composed and multi-node candidates under the same comparability rules**, always including the **direct/native baseline** when one exists. Ranking discloses total delivered amount, sender cost, FX, hops, delivery estimate vs guarantee, reversibility/finality, liquidity risk, required disclosures and evidence freshness. **The simplest eligible route wins unless a longer route's disclosed benefit outweighs its hop costs** — extra hops are a cost center (fees, latency, liquidity, reconciliation, trust boundaries), never a default.

### D7 — Multi-leg execution is a saga, never an atomic transfer

No route is executable because the compiler found it. Before the first effect: revalidate every node/instance/entitlement/quote/recipient; establish every leg's funding and next-leg settlement availability; freeze the immutable route digest. Execution extends **the P22 composite act under the ADR-0016 D13 discipline — extend per-leg, never rewrite; single-leg tests stay green unmodified** (`corridor-dispatch.ts`, `authorization-gate.ts`, `payment-dispatch-runtime.ts`, `mission-execution.ts`): one composite approval covers the plan digest, every consequential leg consumes its own single-use authorization share, carries its own idempotency key `(mission, operation, legIndex, intentDigest)` and its own evidence/reconciliation; **an UNKNOWN result stops all dependent dispatch** (INV-04 — never a second charge because an earlier response timed out); a FAILED leg stops the chain honestly with the custody position recorded; compensation uses only documented provider mechanisms where legally and technically available. Changing any amount, beneficiary, provider, node, fee, FX quote or settlement path invalidates the affected approvals and requires a new preview. **The route compiler still cannot mint authorization** (INV-10; `discoveryPermitsExecution: false` stays a literal).

### D8 — Custody legs are regulated activity

A node that takes custody of, or transmits, funds on behalf of others may itself be conducting regulated financial activity. **A custody-bearing leg is eligible only when the operating node presents its licence/programme approval evidence as a route precondition** (the offer's explicit role + the entitlement evidence chain), and the route records the custody transition at every hop (the `CUSTODY_PARTY_KINDS` continuity law). User-owned merchant balances are never a general pooled PaySwap wallet without an approved custody/safeguarding and licensing model. Where the actual purpose or required legal authority is missing, the result is a **typed exclusion or conditional opportunity** — never a disguised route (ADR-0014 D7 law, generalized).

### D9 — Least disclosure per leg

Each leg receives only the data it needs for its own execution and legal obligations: opaque beneficiary/route references where the provider permits; bank/mobile numbers and KYC documents stay within the provider/authorized node that requires them; every principal-sees-what is recorded. This is **data minimization, not anonymity** — obligated providers and operators still see and retain what law, contract and AML controls require, and D2 makes concealment of originator, beneficiary, beneficial owner, source of funds or purpose from an obligated participant a typed refusal.

### D10 — Country/method/entitlement facts are dated evidence, never flags

Every provider country/method/currency/limit fact is an **evidence-stamped observation with a retrieval date and source URL** (the capability-observation freshness law), re-verified against live provider documentation and real account probes before it is encoded, and again at dispatch where freshness matters. No global "Africa supported" flag exists. The P27-0 research record (`spec/productization/P27-0-CAPABILITY-AND-ELIGIBILITY-MATRIX-2026-11-03.md`) is the initial matrix — and its own two discrepancies against the registration (the Ghana GHS/SWIFT **H2 27** availability label; the unverified-at-station Paystack recipient-type facts) prove why the re-verification law exists.

### D11 — Evidence tiers and operator gates

Every P27 lane ships **key-ready**: harnesses and journeys run against fake/scripted transports in-repo (CONTRACT/COMPOSITION tier) and refuse with **exit 2 without credentials — never a silent pass** (the P22/P26 law); real Sandbox runs are OPERATOR_GATED on credential re-supply through the station secret channel and produce secret-free machine-scanned receipts; **no fixture receipt is ever promoted to real evidence**. LIVE remains refused everywhere (INV-25 at every audited edge); any live activation requires the ADR-0019 bundle, a separate operator order and independent live certification. **No credentials in issues, PRs, chat, fixtures, source, CI logs or evidence bundles** (INV-19; the ADR-0018 push-protection precedent).

### D12 — File-disjoint lane ownership (the no-parallel-kernel law)

There is **one payment kernel, one route engine, one credential broker, one authorization system**. P27 extends existing seams; it never creates parallel ones. Lane ownership (the P27-DEPENDENCY-GRAPH records the machine truth):

## Consequences

PaySwap becomes a payment **capability network and execution orchestrator** — it can connect a Ghanaian business through Paystack and an eligible Stripe-connected business elsewhere and route a genuinely eligible journey between them without pretending either side holds the other's provider account. Reach grows; eligibility does not. The cost is real: more moving parts (offers, quotes, freshness, revocation), more reconciliation surface, more trust boundaries — all of which D6's simplest-eligible-route preference and D7's per-leg saga discipline exist to contain. The honest no-route/conditional-opportunity outcome remains a first-class product surface (ADR-0014 D7). The two research discrepancies the acceptance audit caught (Ghana's H2 27 label vs the registration's Q4 2026; the station-unreachable Paystack docs) are recorded in the capability matrix and stand as the proof that D10's dated-evidence law is load-bearing, not decorative. INV-30 makes the operator's core distinction machine-citable at every future review: **combining capabilities creates paths; it never creates permission.**
